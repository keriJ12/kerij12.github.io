Parola — get Italian words and their articles right in 60 seconds

The trainer I use for my own Italian; the UI is German. One file, three tabs: a scored 60-second challenge, mistakes that file themselves into a review notebook, and mastery tracked per word and per skill — meaning and article separately. Two pressure-free drills for vocabulary and verbs/articles sit beside it. Challenge codes let friends play the identical round and compare results on a share card. Works offline; progress stays in the browser.

01Context

The setting

Living in a German-speaking region, learning Italian, wanting something that takes five minutes a day

Language apps want a login, a subscription and a connection. I wanted: open it, practise, know where I am weak, start there next time. So it is one HTML file that opens from the desktop and works in a phone browser too.

02Problem

Problem statement

“Knowing a word” is two things: its meaning and its article

What beginners in Italian get wrong most is not meaning but il / la / lo / l’. Most vocabulary apps score a word as one item, so a wrong article still counts as “half known”. This trainer splits every word into two skills tracked separately; a noun's article is its own question with its own mastery.

Scope

Built — and deliberately not built

Built

  • 60-second challenge: continuous answering, streak bonus, time penalty for mistakes
  • Mistake notebook: automatic entry; leaves after two consecutive correct answers in that skill
  • Mastery: three consecutive correct per word and skill = 100%, one mistake resets to 0
  • Free vocabulary drill (choice / typing) with your own word lists
  • Verbs in four tenses × 12 verbs, definite and indefinite articles
  • Challenge codes / links, duel results, PNG share card

Deliberately not built

  • Accounts, cloud sync, leaderboards
  • Audio, example sentences, a full course
  • Counting free drills towards challenge mastery
  • Any score dressed up as an exam result

03Design decisions

Decision · Time, not item count

60 seconds, and a mistake costs 3 seconds

Time pressure surfaces the words you know but need too long for — those are the ones to practise. Losing seconds hurts more than losing points, yet one slip does not ruin the round; streaks add 2 points per answer from the second hit, up to 10, rewarding steadiness over speed.

Decision · Automatic notebook

No manual bookmarking; two correct answers in a row to leave

Apps that rely on manually saving mistakes end up with nothing saved. A wrong answer enters the notebook automatically and leaves only after two consecutive correct answers in the same skill, so “I saw it once” never counts as learned. Notebook practice has no timer.

Decision · Mastery resets

Three in a row is 100%; one mistake is back to 0

Strict, but honest: mastery means “stable right now”, not “got it right at some point in history”. The page says so — “a practice value, not an exam result” — and unpractised words count, so the number cannot look better than reality.

Decision · Playing with friends

Challenge codes are seeded, so the same code is the same round

A round is encoded into a code or link; a friend pastes it and gets exactly the same questions in the same order, which makes results comparable. The code carries only an optional nickname and the score, no personal data; the share card is drawn on a canvas as PNG.

Decision · Three independent tabs

Free drills never pollute the challenge data

Vocabulary and verbs/articles are pressure-free practice grounds; their results do not touch challenge mastery, and the page says so. Switching tabs ends a running challenge round, so you cannot step out to look up an answer.

04How it works

Structure

One file, three embedded mini-pages

  • The shell is just three tab buttons; each tab is a complete mini web page embedded in an iframe, isolated from the others but shipped in one file.
  • 60-second challenge: 32 words in 4 topics (everyday, school, food, out and about); unit selection, timer, scoring, notebook, mastery, challenge codes, share card.
  • Vocabulary: free practice by topic, choice or typing, with saved personal word lists.
  • Verbs & articles: presente, passato prossimo, imperfetto, futuro semplice × 12 verbs; definite singular/plural and indefinite articles; cheat sheet included.
Technology

Plain HTML/CSS/JS, about 105 KB

No framework, no libraries. Progress lives in two localStorage keys; if storage is unavailable the page says so instead of breaking. Fonts come from Google Fonts and fall back to system fonts offline. The portfolio version adds only the top navigation bar; content and code are unchanged.

05Limits

Limits

A personal tool at demo scale

32 words and 12 verbs are what I was learning at the time, not a full course. The Italian content is my own compilation, not reviewed by a native speaker. Single user, single device, no accounts or sync; clearing browser data resets everything. Two external links on the grammar tab point to the Treccani encyclopaedia and need a connection.
