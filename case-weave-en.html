WEAVE — From one customer email to a delivered roll of fabric

A handoff workspace for a textile company's customer service, R&D, purchasing, production, quality control, finishing and dispatch teams, designed to keep requests, materials and quality records connected. A browser-based working prototype, from first observations to 269 automated workflow checks. The demo interface is in English.

01Context

The setting

A textile company of about thirty people, where an order passes through seven teams

A customer's request arrives as an email: sometimes "I am looking for a fabric like this", sometimes "I would like to reorder this product". It then moves through customer service, R&D evaluation, purchasing, weaving, greige inspection, external finishing, finished-fabric reinspection, packing and dispatch, until someone confirms the customer has received it. Every stage has an owner, but no one can see the whole thread.

What I observed in that environment: the fabric keeps moving forward, while the information often stays at the previous stage.

Starting point

Not an ERP system — a handoff workspace

The company does not lack systems. It lacks a way for the next person to know what they need to know. So this project deliberately leaves stock deduction, finance and permissions alone, and only deals with how requests, progress, reasons for waiting and quality records travel with an order. Chapter 03 turns that boundary into an explicit list of what is and is not built.

03Problem & scope

Problem statement

What handoffs lack is not judgment — it is the previous stage's record failing to show up

Customer service does not know where the fabric is, purchasing does not know who the material is for, QC does not know where the original defect was. The common thread: the person making a decision does not have the information that decision needs. So the design goal is not to make decisions for people, but to make sure that at every handoff the next person can see what happened before, what is being waited on now, and what comes next.

Scope

What the first version does — and deliberately does not

Built

  • Two entry routes: A (request → development) and B (known product order)
  • Shared downstream flow: partial material deliveries, scheduling, greige inspection, finishing rework, reinspection, dispatch, manual receipt confirmation
  • A reason and follow-up date on every step; waiting is visible
  • Defects travel with the roll and the order into reinspection
  • Cancellation keeps history; only mistaken drafts can be deleted

Deliberately not built

  • Backend, login, real role permissions
  • Stock deduction, order splitting, returns
  • A live AI model, email or supplier integrations
  • Technical-sheet PDF generation
  • Any benefit figures or deployment claims

The "not built" list is stated in the demo's documentation and on screen, so no one mistakes this for a production-ready system.

04Design

Two entry points, one shared back end

Requests start differently, but purchasing to delivery is the same flow

A / REQUEST · DEVELOPMENT

Email structuring → existing-fabric lookup → human review and handoff to R&D → completeness, feasibility, materials, sampling, evaluation, technical sheet → three teams take over. A suitable existing product can be converted directly into a B order, locking the request; an approved technical sheet can create a production order that joins the shared flow.

B / ORDER · KNOWN PRODUCT

Confirm product, colour and quantity → enough finished stock goes straight to picking; otherwise check greige; otherwise confirm warp materials and scheduling. Replacement colour cards are tracked separately and never block a confirmed order; added colours become linked orders.

Decision · Three stock levels

Finished fabric, greige and warp are shown separately; the numbers decide the route

Once a B order is confirmed, a fixed card on the right shows three manually recorded numbers: finished fabric, usable greige, usable warp. Whether there is enough is a business rule, so the route is calculated directly and no model is asked to guess. The same card is visible on the picking, packing and dispatch screens.

Decision · Material arrival

Every purchase belongs to an order, and deliveries can arrive in batches

Each purchase is linked to one order and one customer. Deliveries are recorded in batches with kilograms, a batch reference and manual verification; duplicate batches and quantities above the outstanding amount are blocked. Only when the total is sufficient does production get prompted to resume scheduling, and customer service sees the arrival notice at the same time — no more asking "who is this batch for?".

Decision · Defect traceability

Defects travel with the roll and the order, and cannot be washed away

Defects recorded at greige inspection are tied to the roll number and carried on the delivery note to the finisher. When the fabric returns, the reinspection screen puts the original defects, the finisher's notes and the customer's quality requirements on one page, and each item must be ticked before release. A failed inspection goes back to the same finisher named in the technical sheet; all dispatch and inspection records are kept.

Decision · Waiting is not negligence

Every step records a reason and a follow-up date; elapsed time is for finding bottlenecks

Elapsed time includes waiting for materials, queues and external processing. The machines and follow-up page uses it to show where work is stuck, not to blame a person. Due-date tracking is a rule-based summary and the screen says so explicitly — it is not an AI prediction.

Decision · Cancellation and deletion

Cancellation keeps the full history; only mistaken drafts can be deleted

When a case that has already been purchased, produced or sent out is cancelled, the system lists the corresponding follow-up tasks to work through one by one — but it does not pretend to automatically withdraw a purchase or retrieve fabric from a finisher. Only unsubmitted, unlinked drafts can be moved to a recoverable bin; there is no permanent deletion.

05Testing & fixes

Fixes after review

Two bugs that only showed up by using the prototype

  • No fallback after a passed reinspection. The picking screen after a passed finished-fabric inspection still offered "shortage found → back to production evaluation", which sent an already-approved order back to greige stock confirmation, and the button text did not match where it went. Now only orders that came through finished stock have that button, renamed to say exactly where it leads.
  • Warp status should not be a dropdown. The scheduling screen asked the planner to pick "warp available / shortage" by hand even though required and available kilograms were right there. It is now derived automatically and the error message states the missing kilograms.
Automated checks

13 test files, 269 workflow checks

They run in Node without a browser and call the real event handlers: duplicate and over-quantity deliveries are blocked, a reinspection with one unticked item cannot release, cancellation produces the right follow-up list, a request converted to a B order is locked, data survives a reload. Every change reruns them; the output ships with the demo.

This is not user testing. The prototype has been checked in desktop browsers at 1280 and 900 px; phone widths are not fully verified.

Demo tour

Let a first-time viewer see the key moments in three minutes

06AI boundaries

Principle

Let AI organise. Let people decide.

AI does not make decisions here, but almost every handoff has an input or output where it could stand. I label "implemented" and "reserved" separately, and never describe a simulation as a working model.

IMPLEMENTED · RULE-BASED SIMULATION

Structure messy input

Emails become draft fields with source evidence per field; existing-fabric candidates show why they do not match. Customer service reviews before a case is created.

2 nodes · Local rules and fixed examples, no live model
RESERVED · AI ASSISTANCE

Extract, recognise, draft, summarise

Kilograms and dates from supplier delivery notes, handwritten defect sheets, technical-sheet drafts, per-team handoff summaries, reminder and dispatch messages, "why is this stuck" summaries.

14 nodes · Positions designed, not implemented
DELIBERATELY NOT AI

Rules for facts, people for sign-off

Stock routing, purchase-to-order links and duplicate checks are rules; product confirmation, material verification, quality release and receipt confirmation are human sign-offs.

8 nodes · No model guesses quantities or releases quality

07Outcome & limits

Where it stands

A concept prototype you can walk through — not a deployment result

This project shows how I turn observations from the shop floor into branches, exceptions and confirmation rules, then correct the flow through hands-on feedback. It is a local concept prototype: no business deployment, no benefit figures, no live AI, stock deduction, multi-user sync or logistics integration; role switching is a viewpoint, not access control.

The next step is to watch people who have never seen it use it, and check whether the flow is clear — rather than adding features.
